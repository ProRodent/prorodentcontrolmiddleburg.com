What a rat control visit involves.

Rats in Middleburg tend to show up along the wooded lots that back onto Black Creek, where mature trees give roof rats a route onto the house, and in older detached garages and sheds where Norway rats burrow along the foundation. A technician checks the roofline, the attic, and the yard perimeter, since rat activity is rarely limited to one spot.

Signs include larger droppings than a mouse leaves, gnaw marks on wood and wiring, and rub marks along fence lines or foundation edges where rats travel the same route night after night. Fruit trees, bird feeders, and compost bins near the creek side of a lot are common reasons rats settle in close to a house.

How we remove rats.

Trapping and exclusion are handled together rather than one after the other, since rats are more cautious than mice and a delay between removal and sealing gives them time to find a new way in. Bait stations are placed away from where children or pets can reach them, and every station location is recorded.

  • Exterior stations placed along known travel routes and burrow entrances.
  • Attic and roofline checked for entry through fascia gaps and roof vents.
  • Yard walked for burrows near sheds, woodpiles, and fence lines.
Rats reuse the same route for months if nothing closes it.

Sealing the entry points.

Roof rats get in at the roofline more often than at ground level, so sealing includes checking where tree limbs overhang the roof and trimming back contact points where that is practical. Norway rats are addressed at the foundation, vents, and any gap where a utility line enters the house.

After the visit.

A follow-up visit confirms stations have not been disturbed by anything other than the target animal and checks whether activity has moved to a different part of the property. Tell us if you see rats during the day, since that can mean a larger population than the nighttime signs suggested.

Tell us on the call

  • Whether activity is in the attic, yard or both.
  • Any gnaw marks on wiring or wood.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rat control

Do rats come back to the same spot after being trapped?

New rats can move into a route that previously had activity, which is why sealing the entry point matters as much as the trapping itself.

Are rats active during the day or only at night?

Rats are mostly active at night, though seeing one during the day can be a sign of a larger population nearby.

Are roof rats or Norway rats more common in Middleburg?

Both show up, but roof rats are more common near tree cover and Norway rats are more common at ground level near foundations and sheds.

Can rats chew through walls?

Rats can gnaw through wood, soft metal and some plastics, which is why exclusion uses steel mesh and mortar rather than softer patch materials.

How do I know it's a rat and not a possum?

Rat droppings are smaller and more numerous than possum droppings, and rats leave gnaw marks that possums typically do not.

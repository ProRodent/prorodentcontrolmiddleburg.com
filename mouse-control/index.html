What a mouse control visit involves.

House mice are the most common rodent call in Middleburg, and most calls start the same way: something is heard in a wall or a cabinet at night, or droppings turn up in a kitchen drawer or under the sink. A technician starts by walking the inside and outside of the house, since mice can be active in the attic, the kitchen, and the garage at the same time once a colony is established.

Older homes near the center of town, closer to Main Street Park and Boat Ramp, tend to have more small gaps around original plumbing and window frames. Newer construction further from the creek can still let mice in through garage door gaps and utility penetrations. Either way, the inspection covers the full perimeter, not just the room where activity was noticed.

How we remove mice.

Once entry points and travel routes are mapped, snap traps and enclosed bait stations go in the places mice actually use, based on droppings, grease marks, and gnaw damage rather than guesswork. Kitchens, pantries, and areas behind large appliances get particular attention, since mice nest close to a reliable food source.

  • Traps placed along walls and behind appliances, not out in the open floor.
  • Stations checked and reset on a schedule until activity stops.
  • Any nesting material found in cabinets or wall voids is removed and bagged.
Removal without sealing only resets the clock.

Keeping mice out for good.

A mouse can fit through a gap about the width of a pencil, so sealing focuses on the small stuff that is easy to miss: gaps around pipe penetrations, weep holes, and the bottom corners of garage doors. We use materials mice cannot chew through, not spray foam alone, since foam slows them down for a matter of days at most.

After the visit.

We schedule a follow-up to confirm the traps are clear and that no new activity has started at a different point in the house. If droppings or noise continue after the first visit, tell us exactly where, since a second entry point is common in houses with additions or crawl space access from more than one side.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about mouse control

Do mice avoid houses that are cluttered?

No, clutter tends to attract mice by giving them cover, not the other way around, so reducing clutter near walls and cabinets helps.

Will one dead mouse in a trap mean the problem is solved?

Not always. A follow-up check confirms whether more activity remains before we consider a mouse job finished.

How fast do mice reproduce?

A single pair of mice can produce a large number of offspring in a year, which is why early action matters once you notice the first signs.

Can mice really fit through a pencil-sized hole?

Yes, a mouse can squeeze through a gap about the width of a pencil, which is why gaps around pipes and vents matter as much as obvious holes.

Do ultrasonic repellents work on mice?

We do not rely on ultrasonic devices. Sealing entry points and trapping give results that plug-in repellents do not.

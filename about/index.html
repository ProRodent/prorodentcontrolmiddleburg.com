What happens when you call.

The phone line is answered around the clock, and the first conversation is about your specific situation: what you have seen or heard, where in the house or yard it is happening, and whether children or pets are in the home. From there we talk through same-day scheduling where the calendar allows, and explain what the technician will actually do on arrival rather than leaving you guessing. Equipment and methods we rely on.

Traps and stations are chosen based on species and location rather than a single default option. Exclusion work uses steel mesh, mortar and metal flashing at entry points instead of foam or caulk alone, since rodents can chew through soft materials in a matter of days. Attic and crawl space cleanup follows standard practice for contaminated insulation and droppings, including protective equipment for the technician and safe bagging and disposal rather than dry sweeping, which can spread contamination into the air. Every method used is explained in plain language before and during the visit, so you know what was placed, where, and why.

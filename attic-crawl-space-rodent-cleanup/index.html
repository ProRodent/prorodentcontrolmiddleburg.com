What cleanup involves.

Once rodents have been trapped and entry points sealed, the attic or crawl space itself often still has droppings, urine staining, and nesting material left behind. Cleanup removes all of it, since old droppings can carry health risks if disturbed and dried droppings should never be swept or vacuumed directly.

In Middleburg, older homes with original attic insulation are the most common cleanup calls, since insulation soaked with urine or packed with nesting material loses its effectiveness and holds odor long after the animals are gone.

How the space is handled.

The space is aired out before work begins, droppings are wetted down with a disinfectant rather than swept dry, and contaminated material is bagged and removed. Insulation that is heavily soiled or compacted by nesting is replaced rather than cleaned in place.

  • Droppings and nesting material bagged and removed, never dry-swept.
  • Affected insulation replaced where contamination is significant.
  • Surfaces treated before new insulation or storage goes back in.
A sealed house with a dirty attic is only half finished.

Crawl space specifics.

Homes with a crawl space rather than a slab foundation need extra attention to vapor barriers and any ductwork that runs through the space, since rodents chew through both. Ductwork is inspected for damage during cleanup and flagged separately if repair is needed.

After cleanup.

Once the space is clean, we confirm the seal work is holding before closing it back up. If you plan to add insulation or store items in the attic afterward, wait until we confirm the space is clear so nothing new gets contaminated.

Older Middleburg homes near Black Creek tend to need a closer look at attic ventilation as part of a cleanup, since poor airflow combined with past rodent activity can leave a musty smell behind even after droppings and nesting material are removed. A technician checks vent screens and soffit gaps during cleanup, not just afterward, since those same openings are often how the attic was accessed in the first place.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about attic and crawl space rodent cleanup

How do you know when a cleanup is finished?

The space is checked visually and for odor before we consider a cleanup complete.

Does cleanup include sealing the attic entry point?

Sealing is usually scheduled together with cleanup so the space does not get reinfested right after.

Can I clean droppings myself?

We recommend against dry-sweeping or vacuuming droppings directly, since that can spread contamination into the air.

Is contaminated insulation always replaced?

Only where droppings, urine or nesting material have soaked in. Lightly affected areas can sometimes be cleaned rather than replaced.

Do you wear protective equipment during cleanup?

Yes, respirators and gloves are standard for any attic or crawl space cleanup involving droppings.

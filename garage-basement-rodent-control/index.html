Why garages attract rodents.

Garages across Middleburg, including the detached garages and carports common on the area's larger lots, are an easy first stop for mice and rats before they move into the house itself. Stored boxes, pet food, birdseed, and infrequently used vehicles all give rodents food and cover in one place.

A garage that has ever taken on water during a heavy storm is worth mentioning when you call, since dampness changes where rodents nest and can point to a gap under a door or a foundation crack that also lets water in.

What the visit covers.

The technician checks storage areas, wall-floor junctions, the gap under the main and side doors, and any wiring or plumbing penetrations where the garage meets the house. Traps and stations go where droppings or gnaw marks are actually found.

  • Storage areas checked for nesting in boxes and bins.
  • Door sweeps and thresholds inspected for gaps rodents can use.
  • Shared walls with the house checked for utility penetrations.
A garage is often the door rodents use to reach the rest of the house.

Protecting stored belongings and wiring.

Chewed wiring in a garage is both a fire risk and one of the more expensive problems rodents cause, so exposed wiring runs are checked closely. Boxes and bins are checked from underneath as well as inside, since nesting often starts against the floor where it is least visible.

After the visit.

We recommend keeping stored food, birdseed, and pet food in sealed containers rather than original bags going forward, since that alone removes one of the main reasons rodents settle into a garage in the first place.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Are basements in Middleburg homes common?

True basements are uncommon in this part of Florida; most calls involve garages, crawl spaces, or slab-level storage rooms instead.

Do you check behind stored appliances?

Yes, appliances and large stored items are checked from behind and underneath, common hiding spots for nesting.

Do stored boxes need to be thrown out?

Only if nesting material or contamination is found inside. Most boxes just need to be checked and can stay.

Can rodents damage a garage door opener or wiring?

Yes, chewed wiring in a garage is one of the more common damage calls we see, and it is checked during every garage visit.

Should I keep the garage door closed more often?

Yes, even short periods with the door open give rodents an easy opportunity, especially at night.

What the inspection covers.

An inspection is a full walk of the property before any treatment is decided on: attic, crawl space if the house has one, garage, exterior foundation, roofline, and any interior rooms where activity has been reported. The goal is to identify species, likely entry points, and the scope of the problem before quoting a price.

In Middleburg that usually means checking a house against its distance from Black Creek and any tree cover close to the roof, since those two factors shape whether rats or mice are more likely and where they are probably getting in.

What you get at the end.

The technician walks you through what was found, room by room and entry point by entry point, in plain language, then explains the recommended plan and the price before any work begins. There is no obligation to book a full service after an inspection.

  • Photos of entry points and activity where practical.
  • A written summary of what was found and where.
  • A price for the recommended scope of work, explained before you commit.
An inspection tells you what you are actually dealing with, not a guess.

When to book an inspection instead of a full service.

If you are not sure whether you have mice or rats, or you have noticed a single sign like one dropping or a faint noise, an inspection is the right first step rather than jumping straight to trapping. It is also useful before buying a house with an older crawl space or attic access that has not been checked in years.

After the inspection.

If you decide to move forward, the same technician who did the inspection typically handles the follow-up work, since they already know the layout and the entry points on your property. If you decide to wait, keep an eye on the areas flagged and call back if anything changes.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

What does an inspection actually check?

Attic, foundation, garage, and exterior entry points are all reviewed, along with any signs of current or past activity.

Will I get something in writing?

Yes, you get a written summary of findings and a price for any recommended follow-up work.

Do I have to book a full service after an inspection?

No. The inspection stands on its own, and you decide afterward whether to move forward.

How long does an inspection take?

Most inspections take under an hour, longer if the property has multiple outbuildings or hard-to-reach crawl space access.

Can an inspection happen before I buy a house?

Yes, a pre-purchase inspection is common and gives you a written summary of any activity or entry points found.
